# 🍓 Strawberry Pasta (Aka "How to Make an Albi Cry")

Welcome to the ultimate summer comfort food from Poland. Yes, it is sweet. Yes, it uses sour cream and sugar on pasta. And yes, we intentionally overcook the pasta just a little bit and break the spaghetti in half if we feel like it. *Buon appetito!*

* **Prep time:** 10 minutes
* **Cook time:** 12 minutes (plus 3 extra minutes past *al dente* just to be safe)
* **Servings:** 2 horrified Italians (or 2 happy Poles)

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

* **250 g** Pasta (Fusilli, Penne, or **Spaghetti broken cleanly in half**)
* **400 g** Fresh strawberries, hulled
* **3–4 tbsp** Full-fat sour cream (18% *śmietana*) or Greek yogurt
* **2–3 tbsp** White sugar (or vanilla sugar for maximum chaos)
* **1 pinch** Salt (barely any in the water, we aren't making ocean soup)
* **Optional garnish:** Crumbled white *twaróg* (curd cheese) on top instead of Parmigiano!

---

## 👨‍🍳 Instructions

1. **Boil the pasta (The Wrong Way):** 
   Bring a pot of water to a boil. If using spaghetti, look your Italian friend dead in the eye and **snap the dry noodles in half** before dropping them in. Cook until soft—forget *al dente*, we want tender childhood nostalgia here.

2. **Make the strawberry "ragù":** 
   While the pasta is boiling, throw the fresh strawberries into a bowl. Using a potato masher or a fork, mash them up until chunky and juicy. (Leave a few larger pieces for texture).

3. **Add the dairy and sugar:** 
   Dump the sour cream and white sugar directly into the mashed strawberries. Stir until it turns into a bright pink, sweet, creamy sauce. Taste it—if it doesn't taste like dessert yet, add another spoonful of sugar.

4. **Commit the final sin (Rinse the pasta):** 
   Drain the pasta in a colander and rinse it under water so it doesn't stick. Don't save the pasta water! Just pour the pasta onto deep plates and immediately ladle the cold, sweet pink strawberry-cream mixture right on top so it gets lukewarm.

5. **Serve:** 
   Top with crumbled sweet cottage cheese (*twaróg*) and serve as a main course for dinner, followed by a cappuccino at 4:00 PM.

---

### 💡 Chef's Notes for Italian Guests
* Do **not** add garlic, olive oil, black pepper, or basil.
* Do **not** ask where the *soffritto* is.
* If an Italian passes out at the table, wave a basil leaf under their nose and remind them that Iga Świątek eats this to win Grand Slams.
